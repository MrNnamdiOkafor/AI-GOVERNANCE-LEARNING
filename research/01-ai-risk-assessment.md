# AI Risk Assessment

## 1. Research Question

How should an organisation assess the risk  associated with an AI system that processes personal data?

## 2. Why AI Risk Assessment Matters

AI systems can create different types of risks depending on how they are designed, the data they use, the decisions they support and the context in which  they are deployed.

A structured  risk assessment can help an organisation identify these risks before and during the use of an AI system.

### 3. Key Risk Areas

### Data Protection Risk

An AI system may process personal data in ways that create risks to individual's privacy and data protection rights.

### Bias and Discrimination

AI systems may produce unfair outcomes if the data, design or deployment process introduces or amplifies bias.

### Transparency  Risk

Individuals and organisations may have difficulty understanding how an AI system uses data or produces particular outputs.

### Security Risk

AI systems may create risks involving unauthorised access, data leakage,  manipulation or other security vulnerabilities.

### Accountability Risk

An organisation needs to establish who is responsible for decisions concerning the development, deployment and monitoring of an AI system.

## 4. Questions an Organisation Should Ask

- What is the purpose of the AI system?
-  What data does the system process?
-  Does the system process personal data?
-  What could go wrong?
-  Who could be affected?
-  How serious could the consequences be?
-  How likely are the identified risks to occur?
-  What controls are available to reduce those risks?
-  Who is responsible for monitoring the system?
-  How will the organisation review the risks over time?

 ## 5. Possible Governance Controls

Potential controls may include:

-  Data protection impact assessments
-  AI risks assessments
-  Human oversights
-  Access controls
-  Documentation
-  Testing and validation
-  Monitoring and auditing
-  Incident management
-  Staff training
-  Periodic review

## 6. My Initial Analysis

My current understanding is that AI risk assessment should not be treated as a one-time exercise.

The risk associated with an AI system may change as the system, data, users and operating environment change.

Effective AI governance therefore requires an ongoing process of identifying, assessing, mitigating and monitoring risk.
